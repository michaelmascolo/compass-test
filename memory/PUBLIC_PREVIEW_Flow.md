# Public Preview — Flow Design (Aha C, refined)
## Organizing principle: an authentic moment of learner-OWNED understanding

> Refined north star: not "Compass withheld the answer," but **"the understanding became genuinely mine because Compass created the conditions under which I built it myself."** Every element below is judged by one test:
>
> **THE OWNERSHIP TEST — Does this make the final understanding *more the learner's own*, or does it make Compass look clever?** If the latter, cut or rewrite it.
>
> Decisions are canonical; dialogue is illustrative (voice/stance only). No implementation here. Runs on the existing engine; anti-coauthoring absolute; one target + one question per turn.

## The single insight the learner will construct (the destination)
Across all branches the learner is led to build, in their own words, one projectable principle about composing:
> **"A reader won't accept what I think until I've given them a reason to need it."**
Chosen because it is (a) essay-grounded, (b) reachable from any first sentence in ~4 minutes, and (c) **projectable** into any future writing situation — which the transfer beat requires. Compass never states this sentence; the learner does.

## Design test applied to each element

### 1) The Seed — manufacture ownership at t=0
Invitation asks for **a belief the learner actually holds, in plain words** — because the closing insight can only feel *theirs* if it is built from *their* meaning, not a supplied prompt.
- Illustrative: *"Tell me one thing you believe should change — about anything. One sentence, your own words. Don't make it sound like an essay."*
- **Why it serves ownership:** the learner supplies the raw material; everything downstream is *their* idea maturing, so the eventual principle is self-evidently theirs.
- **Runway requirement:** it must yield a *claim with stake*, not a neutral topic. If the learner gives a bare topic, Turn 1-branch-A converts it into a stake (below) — that conversion is itself early ownership.
- **Rejected alternatives:** pasting a student sample (the insight would be *about someone else's* writing, not owned); "write a thesis" (jargon-first breaks meaning-before-convention and pre-loads Compass's frame).

### 2) Branching — many roads, one owned destination
Compass reads the seed as a *reader* and takes the single step that advances *the learner's* thinking toward the destination. Branch by what they actually wrote:

- **A · Bare topic / no stake** ("School lunches.") → make them feel the reader's blank: *"If that's all I read, I know your subject but not what you want me to think. What should change — and who'd have to do something differently?"* → the learner supplies the stake. (Ownership: they created the claim.)
- **B · Real but unmotivated claim** ("Phones should be banned in class.") → surface the resistant reader: *"Someone who disagrees just read that. What are they thinking right now?"* → the learner voices the reader's resistance themselves. (Ownership: they discovered the need, unprompted.)
- **C · Already strong, motivated claim** → **restraint path.** Do NOT invent a flaw (that would make the experience Compass's, not theirs). Consolidate what they did and push one genuine step: *"You've already given a reader a reason to lean in. How did you do that — what did that opening give them?"* → the learner articulates the mechanism they used. (Ownership: they name their own competence.)

All three converge on the same felt discovery — *a reader needs a reason before they'll accept a point* — reached from the learner's own sentence, so the branch is invisible and the insight is theirs regardless of entry point.

### 3) Scaffolding — shrink the step, never supply the content
The scaffolding ladder is the *guarantor* of ownership: if Compass ever supplies the idea, the understanding stops being the learner's.
- **Rung 1 (default):** ask the one reader-question that makes the gap visible.
- **Rung 2 (if the learner stalls):** narrow the lens — a smaller, concrete reader (*"imagine your most skeptical colleague read only that line"*).
- **Rung 3 (if still stuck):** lower the stakes — ask for a reaction, not an answer (*"just tell me how that reader would feel — bored, unconvinced, curious?"*).
- **Never:** supply the sentence, the fix, or a fill-in frame. If the learner asks Compass to write it: *"I could hand you a line, but then the understanding would be mine, not yours. Tell me what your reader is missing, and you'll have it."* — this refusal exists solely to keep the insight owned.
- Convention words (thesis, hook, context) appear **only after** the learner has felt the need — and even then as a name for *what the learner just did*, never as an upfront rule.

### 4) The Ending — transfer by PROJECTION, learner-constructed
The centerpiece. Compass does not summarize and does not praise. It asks the learner to **carry the discovery into a future, unfamiliar writing situation** and say what they will do — turning a realization into an owned, portable principle.
- Illustrative: *"Next week you'll have to write about something your reader already disagrees with — before they've read a word of yours. Knowing what you just noticed here, what will you have to do in your opening lines?"*
- **Why projection, not restatement:** restating a rule can be mimicry; *projecting* it into a new situation is only possible if the learner actually owns the understanding. The learner's forward-looking answer is the authentic proof — to themselves — that the insight is theirs.
- Compass lets the learner's projection stand as the final word (no wrap-up, no "great job"). Silence here is deliberate: it leaves the ownership with the learner.

### 5) The Exit — pull from what they just did
One in-character line that extends *their* projection into their real work: *"That's exactly the move your students struggle with most. Want to bring a real assignment and keep going?"* The pull is a consequence of the owned moment, not a feature list.

## Reliability engineering (produce the moment within 3–5 min)
- **Time-box:** target the projection beat by exchange 4–5; hard soft-cap ~6 exchanges. The arc, not coverage, is protected.
- **Runway protection is the top priority:** if a branch risks stalling, Compass shrinks the step (scaffolding ladder) rather than adding a new topic. Never open a second instructional thread in the preview.
- **Failure modes & recovery (all kept in character):**
  - *One-word / off-topic:* re-anchor to a real belief; no lecture.
  - *"Write it for me":* the ownership-preserving refusal above (also the anti-coauthoring showcase).
  - *Hostile / "prove you beat ChatGPT":* never defend or compare; keep advancing *their* thinking — the contrast self-demonstrates.
  - *Expert who nails it immediately:* restraint branch C, then straight to projection — do not manufacture a problem to "have something to teach."
- **The one thing that must never happen:** Compass stating the principle for the learner. That single act converts an owned insight into a delivered answer and forfeits the entire preview.

## Success criterion (single, behavioral)
The preview succeeds if and only if **the learner, in their own words, projects the insight into a future writing situation** — i.e., describes what they'll do for a reader they haven't met yet. Everything else (diagnosis, restraint, anti-coauthoring, meaning-before-convention) is instrumentation toward that one owned moment. If it happens, the preview worked even if secondary strengths were barely visible; if it doesn't, the preview failed even if every strength was on display.
