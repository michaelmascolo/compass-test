## Evidence vs. interpretation — the central M8 distinction

- **Strong evidence / weak interpretation (1–4):** the engine honored the data/quotation and named the developmental edge as INTERPRETATION — the draft asserts 'this proves X' without helping the reader see WHY the evidence means X. Instruction targeted the evidence→meaning link, never 'add more evidence'.
- **Weak evidence / strong interpretation (5–7):** interpretation honored; the edge named as grounding the interpretive claim in concrete support the student already has — without the AI supplying any evidence.
- **Missing evidence (23–25) / unsupported claims (28–29):** correctly read as claim-without-grounding; the student was invited to consider what would ground the point (a writing/reader-understanding move), not handed content. applies=false is legitimate here (no support present yet).
- **Irrelevant evidence (26–27):** support present but not connected to purpose; the engine surfaced the relevance/connection gap rather than counting pieces.

## Forms of evidence honored across purposes

Statistics/research (persuasion), process descriptions & demonstrations (explanation), textual features/patterns (analysis), sensory details/actions/dialogue (narrative), and memories/experiences (reflection) were all recognized as support — teaching evidence as a purpose-relative communicative resource, not a fixed category.
