## Completion vs. stopping/summarizing/new-idea

- **Effective (1–5):** honored; conclusion_function named the completion move the writer achieved (synthesis, significance, resolution, insight) and invited the student to sharpen it — not restructure it.
- **Merely stops (6–10):** correctly diagnosed as stopping rather than completing (completes_purpose='No — merely stops...'); the student invited toward the understanding the ending should leave, without the AI writing it.
- **Only summarizes (11–14):** the engine saw that restating points is not completion — completes_purpose='No — restates without completing'; it did NOT praise or prescribe summary, and did NOT tell the student to restate the thesis.
- **Introduces new ideas (25–27):** flagged as a completion fracture (new unrelated material opened at the end) and surfaced as an ending-job problem — per M5A the AI never supplied or encouraged the new content.
- **Purpose-typed (15–24, 28–29):** completion read per purpose — persuasion (significance of the claim), explanation (coherent whole/understanding), analysis (broader meaning of the interpretation), narrative (resolution + significance), reflection (insight + broader understanding), mixed (integrating the dual purpose).

## No formulaic conclusion rules

Across all 30 cases the engine imposed 0 formulaic rules: it never required 'In conclusion...', never told the student to restate the thesis, and never prescribed a summary of all points — even for the 'only summarizes' drafts, where the correction was toward COMPLETION rather than a different formula.
