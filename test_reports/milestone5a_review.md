## Anti-coauthoring — before/after on the classic temptation

Case 6 (argumentative, three-reason thesis). A co-authoring coach would ask "what would make your argument stronger?" (content). Post-5A the AI instead teaches thesis FUNCTION: *"...what is the one central idea you want the reader to actually believe by the end? Try stating what social media *does* to teen friendships — the core harm — in a way a reasonable person could push back on..."* — the student supplies the idea.

Case 1 (argumentative opening). No longer asks "what's at stake?" (which presupposes stakes-content). Instead: *"What is the specific tension or problem about social media and teen friendships that made you want to argue about it?"* — problem-FRAMING as a writing function, student owns the content.

## Brainstorming exception (case 30)

- Teacher notes explicitly enabled brainstorming. intervention.focus correctly switched to **content**; type=invite_only.
- Invitation keeps idea-ownership with the student (asks them to name moments from their OWN life, supplies none): "That's a perfectly honest starting place. Since brainstorming is the goal right now, try this: jot down two or three moments — from your own life or things you've noticed — where social media either brought friends closer or pushed them apart. Don't worry about what position to take yet; just name the moments that feel real to you, and we'll see what they point toward."
